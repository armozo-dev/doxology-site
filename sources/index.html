Sources

The Church's own books

Every prayer, hymn and reading in Doxology Way comes from a historic book of its own tradition, and every source is named in the app. All of them are in the public domain: published in the United States before 1931, or translated for the app from texts centuries old.

Scripture

  • King James Version (1769 text), with the Apocrypha
  • American Standard Version (1901)
  • World English Bible, dedicated to the public domain by its translators
  • Douay-Rheims, Challoner revision (1749–52), with the American edition of 1899 for the lessons from the deuterocanonical books
  • The Septuagint, translated by Sir Lancelot Brenton (London, 1851)

Orthodox

  • Service Book of the Holy Orthodox-Catholic Apostolic Church, Isabel F. Hapgood (1906): the prayers, the offices and the table of readings; the weekday readings follow the Russian lectionary tables of Skvortsov (1904) and Grechulevich (1853)
  • The Great Horologion (1851): the saints' troparia and kontakia for the whole year, translated afresh for this app from the Greek, shown with the Greek
  • The General Menaion, translated by Nicholas Orloff (London, 1899): the common hymns of the saints, checked against the printed pages
  • The Akathist Hymn, W. J. Birkbeck and G. R. Woodward (1917)
  • Book of Divine Prayers and Services (New York, 1938), for the Sunday Hypakoe and the saints' own Epistles and Gospels; a United States copyright never renewed
  • St. John of Kronstadt, My Life in Christ, translated by E. E. Goulaeff (1897)
  • St. Maximus the Confessor, Chapters on Love, translated for this app from the Greek of the Philokalia (Athens, 1893)

Catholic

  • The Roman Breviary, translated by John, Marquess of Bute (1879; 1908 edition): Lauds, the Hours, Vespers, Compline, the hymns and the saints' prayers
  • The Epistles and Gospels for Pulpit Use (1893) and the Missale Romanum (1889): the readings of the Mass
  • Manual of Prayers (Baltimore, 1888) and F. X. Lasance (1914): the devotions; St. Alphonsus Liguori's Stations of the Cross (1887)
  • Thomas à Kempis, The Following of Christ, translated by Richard Challoner; St. Francis de Sales, Introduction to a Devout Life; St. Alphonsus Liguori, The Glories of Mary (New York, 1888)

Anglican

  • The Book of Common Prayer (1928): Morning and Evening Prayer, the collects, and the table of daily lessons
  • The Hymnal of the Episcopal Church (1916)
  • Jeremy Taylor, Holy Living and Holy Dying, The Worthy Communicant (London, 1853) and Sermons (New York, 1874)

Lutheran

  • Common Service Book of the Lutheran Church (1917): Matins, Vespers, the collects, the hymns, and the Epistles and Gospels of the Sundays and festivals
  • Luther's Small Catechism, in its historic English translation
  • Johann Arndt, True Christianity (Philadelphia, 1868); Johann Gerhard, Sacred Meditations, translated by C. W. Heisler (Philadelphia, 1896)

Evangelical

  • The Methodist Hymnal (1905)
  • C. H. Spurgeon, Morning by Morning (1865) and Evening by Evening (1868)
  • Matthew Henry, Exposition of the Old and New Testament

The Fathers on Scripture

  • St. John Chrysostom, homilies on the Gospels and Epistles, in the nineteenth-century Library of the Fathers translations
  • St. Thomas Aquinas, Catena Aurea, translated under John Henry Newman (1841–45)
  • St. Cyril of Alexandria, commentaries on Luke and John
  • The Ante-Nicene Fathers and The Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers (1885–1900): Augustine, Ambrose, Jerome, Basil, Gregory of Nyssa, Cassian and others on the day's readings, each copied from the printed volumes

The saints and history

The short lives and histories on Today are written for the app, about two thousand of them, each from public-domain works that it names: the Roman Martyrology (1916), Delehaye's Synaxarium of Constantinople (1902), Butler's Lives of the Saints, the Catholic Encyclopedia (1907–14), the eleventh edition of the Encyclopaedia Britannica (1911), and the ancient histories of Eusebius, Bede and others. Notes on the hymns come from Julian's Dictionary of Hymnology (1907).

How the texts were prepared

Texts are taken from scans of the printed books, many from the Princeton Theological Seminary and other libraries through the Internet Archive, and checked against the page images, not typed from memory. Hymns and prayers are given as their book prints them. Where a text is newly translated for the app, it says so, and the original is shown alongside.
